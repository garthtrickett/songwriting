# Songwriting

The musical vocabulary for writing math rock through relative notes, chords,
interacting patterns, rhythm, and harmonic structure.

## Language

**Song**:
A composition comprising musical sections and their arrangement, with parts,
rhythm, and harmony.

**Section**:
A reusable passage such as a verse, chorus, or bridge, comprising bars,
musical placements, phrases, and lyrics.

**Section occurrence**:
A particular appearance of a section in the arrangement, carrying that
section’s music and annotations.

**Arrangement**:
The ordered sequence of section occurrences in a song.

**Phrase**:
A named musical span within a section, expressing a connected idea.

**Lyric span**:
Words attached to a span of music within a section, optionally associated
with a phrase and a part.

**Part**:
A musical contribution such as a vocal melody, bass line, guitar accompaniment,
or drum pattern.

**Voice**:
An independently timed musical line within a part; multiple voices may overlap
on the same instrument.

**Pattern**:
A reusable musical idea with events and a defined cycle duration, encompassing
riffs and motifs without requiring a whole number of bars.

**Pattern occurrence**:
A placement of a pattern in a part, with a start, repeat span, and starting
position within its cycle. It may belong to a section or span the song independently.

**Variation**:
An explicitly differentiated version of a musical idea, such as a riff with a
changed ending, rhythm, or pitch sequence.

**Phase**:
The position within a repeating pattern's cycle at a given point in song time.

**Polyrhythm**:
Concurrent rhythmic divisions of a shared span, such as three evenly spaced
attacks against two.

**Polyrhythm span**:
A declared shared span in which independent voices have specified rhythmic
subdivisions; the performed music may depart from the declared grid.

**Pattern grouping**:
The ordered pulses within a riff's own cycle, independent of the song's bars.

**Rhythmic displacement**:
Moving a musical idea in time while preserving its internal rhythm.

**Note**:
A pitched musical element expressed relative to a tonal centre by degree,
alteration, and octave.

**Chord**:
A collection of one or more notes treated as a harmonic unit. The app permits a
single-note collection, although ordinary musical usage generally implies several notes.

**Musical event**:
An occurrence of a note, chord, drum hit, or rest with a musical start and duration.

**Rest**:
Intentional silence within a voice, distinct from an unfinished gap or a command
to silence every voice on an instrument.

**Chord performance**:
The attacks and releases that realise a chord's member notes, whether together,
staggered, or sustained for different lengths.

**Accent**:
An emphasis on a musical attack relative to surrounding attacks.

**Articulation**:
The manner in which a note is attacked, sustained, or released, such as staccato
or muted playing.

**Pedal note**:
A sustained or reiterated note continuing beneath or above changing harmony.

**Bar**:
A span of musical time governed by a meter. A pickup or intentionally shortened
bar may occupy less than that meter's full duration.

**Meter**:
The organisation of musical time into recurring groups, expressed through a
time signature and beat grouping; it may change between bars.

**Beat grouping**:
The ordered grouping of a bar's note units into pulses, such as `2+2+3` in
`7/8`; different groupings can share the same time signature.

**Tempo**:
The rate of a specified musical beat unit, such as quarter notes or dotted
quarter notes, expressed in beats per minute.

**Voicing**:
The choice and octave placement of the notes expressing a chord.

**Tonal centre**:
The reference tonic against which relative pitches and harmonies are understood.

**Degree**:
A numbered pitch position relative to the tonal centre, using the major scale as
the reference; alterations express lowered or raised positions.

**Mode**:
The scale context of a passage, such as major or minor, without changing the
fixed reference used to describe its degrees.

**Roman numeral**:
A relative interpretation of a chord's root and quality, with additional notation
for relationships such as applied chords; context may permit multiple interpretations.

**Playback tonic**:
The absolute pitch chosen to hear a relative composition in a particular key.

**Guitar arrangement**:
A playable realisation of the relative composition for a chosen guitar tuning
and capo position, including fingerings and technique markings.
